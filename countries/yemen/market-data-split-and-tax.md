**CBY-Aden market data (scale of the licensed perimeter)**
- **Q3 2024:** 7 e-wallets; YER 29.94bn of e-money issued in the quarter (Q2: 7.38bn; Q1: 9.57bn); 581,075 wallet subscribers. The "number of payment service providers" tile reads 2 (my reading of the extracted text; moderate confidence). — [CBY-Aden PSD payments bulletin Q3 2024](https://cby-ye.com/files/677c2e75a4d8c.pdf)

## Key question 2: How has the split between the Aden and Sana'a central banks (including the 2024 dispute over bank headquarters and the unified money transfer network) changed which payment and transfer providers may operate where, and what does a platform serving both areas face?

[…]

### Cited Findings
- **Background [PRE-2023]:** the central bank was relocated to Aden in Sept 2016. From Dec 2019 the Houthis banned the "new" rial notes printed by Aden, which split the currency zones and pushed e-rial usage in the north. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **Unified network (UNMONEY):**
  - Its legal basis traces to CBY-Aden Decision No. 18 of 2020.
  - The company was publicised with capital of YER 5bn. — [Al-Ayyam](https://www.alayyam.info/news/9QC6JW9G-VE3NRB-0C0C)
  - It was reported as officially activated in February 2024. — [Sana'a Center search summary of Yemen Review 2025; activation date not verified on a fetched page]
  - In March 2024 the Sana'a exchangers' association told exchange companies and networks not to deal with UNMONEY or with banks unlicensed by Sana'a. — [Yeni Yemen](https://yeniyemen.net/p-271984)
  - CBY-Aden later restructured UNMONEY. It discussed with banks creating a World Bank-backed fast-payment company and handing UNMONEY to the banking sector. — [Al-Wahdawi](https://alwahdawi.net/news-38769); [Belqees](https://belqees.net/locals/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%B2%D9%8A-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%AC%D8%AA%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A8%D9%85%D9%85%D8%AB%D9%84%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%86%D9%88%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%AD%D8%AB-%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81%D9%88%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D9%87%D9%8A%D9%83%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%AD%D8%AF%D8%A9) (dates not stated)
- **Headquarters dispute, 2024:**
  - In spring 2024 CBY-Aden gave Sana'a-headquartered banks 60 days to move their head offices to Aden.
  - On 30 May 2024 it suspended dealings with 6 banks for non-compliance with AML/CFT rules. — [Al-Arabiya, 30 May 2024](https://www.alarabiya.net/aswaq/2024/05/30/-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%B2%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%82%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%84-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A8%D9%86%D9%88%D9%83-%D9%84%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%A7-%D8%A8%D9%80-%D8%AC%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9-)
  - CBY-Sana'a retaliated by banning 13 banks in government areas, including Al-Ahli and CAC, as unlicensed by Sana'a. — [Al-Masdar Online](https://almasdaronline.com/articles/295758) (exact date not confirmed)
- **26 June 2024:** CBY-Aden issued Decision 23/2024 (domestic cash transfers only via UNMONEY; licensed e-wallets and PSPs exempt within ceilings) and Circular CBY/L/282/2024 (ban on dealing with 12 Sana'a-based wallets). — [CBY news 706](https://cby-ye.com/news/706); [Circular](https://cby-ye.com/files/667c32dabd223.pdf)
- **23 July 2024 de-escalation:** the UN envoy announced that the government and the Houthis agreed to cancel "all the recent decisions and procedures against banks by both sides" and to refrain from similar measures. Saudi Arabia played a significant role; Yemenia flights were part of the package. — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/houthis-yemeni-government-reach-financial-de-escalation-deal); [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/yemen-s-warring-rivals-agree-to-de-escalate-economic-conflict-un/3283264)
- **[CONTESTED] Status of the 12-wallet ban.** Al-Mushahid (15 June 2025) reports that CBY-Aden later reversed the ban after Saudi pressure. It gives no date or instrument. The circular is still posted on cby-ye.com. — [Al-Mushahid](https://almushahid.net/131223/); [CBY news 703](https://cby-ye.com/news/703)
- **15 March 2025:** after the US FTO designation of Ansarallah, CBY-Aden announced that 8 banks had notified it in writing that they were moving their head offices and operations from Sana'a to Aden "to avoid strict US sanctions". The banks: Tadhamon, Al-Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance, Shamil Bank of Yemen & Bahrain, Islamic Bank of Yemen, Saba Islamic, Yemen Gulf Bank, Yemen Commercial Bank, Al-Amal Microfinance. — [CBY-Aden notice, 15 Mar 2025](https://english.cby-ye.com/news/187); [Sana'a Center Yemen Review Apr–Jun 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056)
  - A CBY-Aden official later said three banks had fully complied. — [same source](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056)
- **IBY stayed in Sana'a.** Treasury said it "continues to refuse" to relocate when it designated the bank on 17 Apr 2025. — [US Treasury sb0092](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0092)
- **22 June 2025:** CBY-Sana'a ordered financial institutions to suspend dealings with Al-Kuraimi and liquidate their balances within 15 days. Exchange companies and networks got the same order. A run on cash followed. — [Sana'a Center Yemen Review Apr–Jun 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056); [Al-Nidaa](https://www.alndaa.net/102197/central-bank-sanaa-suspends-dealings-alkuraimi-bank-gives)
  - Al-Kuraimi held 91% of active microfinance depositors (3.3m clients) in 2023 and runs the M-Floos wallet. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056); [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **2025 consolidation:**
  - July 2025: the Houthis seized Yemen Gulf Bank and appointed a judicial custodian.
  - Rafidain closed its Sana'a branch; QNB agreed to liquidate its Sana'a branch (mid-June 2025).
  - Through 2025 CBY-Aden revoked dozens of exchange-company and exchange-shop licences for currency speculation.
  - 16 Oct 2025: Governor al-Maabqi called relocating the banking system to Aden "a pivotal step".
  - October 2025: CBY-Aden banned foreign currency for domestic purchases.
  - Source: [Sana'a Center Yemen Review Oct–Dec 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/oct-dec-2025/26135)
- **Exchange-rate gap:** in Q2 2026 the government-area rial traded at about YR1,562–1,573 per US$, while old rials in Houthi areas traded at YR531–534 per US$. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
  - Early March 2026: exchangers in government areas capped hard-currency sales at SAR 100 or US$50 per person per day, and banks capped electronic transfers into rials at SAR 200 or US$50 per day. — [Sana'a Center Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962)
- **Interoperability:**
  - Wallets are poorly linked across the divide.
  - Al-Kuraimi offers region-specific accounts because of the currency split.
  - Some Sana'a government fee payments are restricted to CAC Bank.
  - CBY-Sana'a has barred dealings with 21 exchange companies.
  - Source: [Al-Mushahid, 15 Jun 2025](https://almushahid.net/131223/)
- **Rival infrastructure:** the Houthis run their own Fast Payment System in their areas, while CBY-Aden licensed YPCC in May 2026. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
- **4 April 2026:** the Houthi Ministry of Economy, Industry and Trade revoked the licences of 4,225 commercial agencies, later granting 90 days' grace. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
- **Mid-June 2026:** the Aden Public Prosecutor froze all STC funds at banks, financial institutions and exchange companies, and CBY-Aden ordered immediate reporting of STC-linked accounts. This shows that political freezes now also apply inside government areas. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)

## Key question 5: What tax obligations (general sales tax rates, digital-services rules, invoicing) and consumer-protection or e-commerce rules apply to platform-mediated sales? (Including data rules.)

[…]

### Cited Findings
- **GST statute:**
  - Law No. 19 of 2001 imposes GST on local sales and imports of goods and services. — [WTO accession copy of Law 19/2001](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/acc_e/yem_e/WTACCYEM18A1_LEG_6.pdf); [Yemen NIC Law 19/2001](https://yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=11733)
  - Law No. 42 of 2005 (18 July 2005) amended it, setting the general rate at 5% [PRE-2023]. — [Yemen NIC Law 42/2005](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=19343); [WTO copy](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/acc_e/yem_e/WTACCYEM18A1_LEG_6.pdf)
  - Law No. 7 of 2020 (3 May 2020; government side) amended the GST law on cancelling tax assessments for certain periods. — [Yemen Tax Authority](https://tax.gov.ye/7-2020/)
- **Grant Thornton indirect-tax guide (1 Jan 2022) [PRE-2023]:**
  - Rates: standard 5%; telecoms and mobile 10%; exports zero-rated; some items exempt.
  - Registration: mandatory at YER 50m annual taxable turnover (taxable plus exempt sales); importers, exporters and distribution agents register regardless of size.
  - Non-resident suppliers without an establishment must appoint a local representative; otherwise the resident customer pays.
  - Unregistered recipients of imported services remit within 30 days.
  - "No specific legislation" exists for non-resident digital or electronic services.
  - Tax invoice (paper or electronic, serially numbered, in duplicate) must show seller and buyer names, addresses and registration or ID numbers, description, value, rate, tax and total.
  - Returns are monthly, due within 21 days.
  - Penalties: 2% per month (capped at 20%) for late filing or payment, plus 1% per month on assessed tax; doubled for repeat offences within a year.
  - No e-invoicing or real-time reporting.
  - Source: [Grant Thornton, Indirect tax – Yemen](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/indirect-tax-guide/indirect-tax---Yemen/)
- **Sana'a side:** the Sana'a government approved draft amendments to the income-tax, GST and customs laws and sent them to its parliament. — [Yemen Eco](https://yemeneco.org/archives/57418) (undated in the search result; probably about 2023; content of the rates not found)
  - In 2026 the Houthis intensified taxation (state taxes, "war-effort" taxes, mandatory donations). — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
  - A Houthi security official oversees fee and tax collection from exchange and commercial companies (OFAC, 11 Sept 2025). — [US Treasury sb0243](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0243)
- **Government-side customs:**
  - Presidential Decree No. 11 (late Oct 2025) moved customs to the market rate (about YR1,630 per US$, from YR700) and required central revenue deposits at CBY-Aden. — [Sana'a Center Oct–Dec 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/oct-dec-2025/26135)
  - In mid-May 2026 the customs dollar for non-essential goods was liberalised from YR750 to YR1,550. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
  - The 2026 state budget (March 2026) projects revenue of YR3.96tn, 41.5% from taxes. — [Sana'a Center Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962)
